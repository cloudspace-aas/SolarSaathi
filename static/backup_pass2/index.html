Answers to Common Solar Questions

Everything you need to know about rooftop solar sizing, subsidies, payback periods, and our calculation methodology.

SolarSaathi is an open educational web application designed for Indian homeowners and college hackathon evaluation. It provides indicative rooftop solar capacity recommendations, estimates PM Surya Ghar financial assistance, calculates electricity bill reductions, projects 10-year investment returns, and estimates ecological benefits using transparent, configurable mathematical models.

Results are preliminary engineering estimates. SolarSaathi uses regional peak sun hour benchmarks (4.1 to 5.8 kWh/m²/day), a standard 80% performance ratio, and benchmark turnkey installation costs (₹55,000/kW). Actual generation will depend on roof tilt, orientation, local shadowing from trees or adjacent buildings, module quality, and your local DISCOM's tariff slabs and net-metering regulations.

No. SolarSaathi is an independent educational tool and cannot approve subsidies or confirm official eligibility. Subsidy approval depends on registration via the National Portal for PM Surya Ghar, verification of your electricity consumer number, installation through an empanelled vendor, bi-directional net meter installation, and physical inspection by your local distribution company.

Household power consumption varies throughout the year due to weather, seasonal air conditioning, and water heating. By averaging three consecutive bills, SolarSaathi calculates a more representative baseline demand, preventing under-sizing in winter or severe over-sizing based on peak summer heatwaves.

Modern high-efficiency solar photovoltaic panels typically require approximately 100 square feet of shadow-free rooftop area per 1 kW of installed capacity. A standard 3 kW residential array requires around 300 sq. ft of unshaded terrace or roof space.

A well-sized solar system can offset 70% to 90% or more of your energy consumption units. However, monthly utility bills usually still include fixed meter charges, electricity duty, and minimum connection fees required by your state electricity regulatory commission, even when net energy consumption is zero.

The simple payback period is the time (in years) required for cumulative electricity bill savings to equal your initial net capital investment (gross cost minus government subsidy). In most sun-rich Indian states, rooftop solar recovers its net cost within 3 to 5 years, after which the system generates virtually free electricity for the remainder of its 25-year design lifespan.

When online, SolarSaathi connects to the free Open-Meteo API to display current direct solar radiation and cloud cover as an illustrative snapshot of real-time solar intensity. These live observations are purely informational—our long-term generation, financial, and payback calculations rely strictly on verified multi-year regional insolation benchmarks.

No. SolarSaathi is designed to run entirely locally without requiring user accounts, passwords, paid cloud APIs, AWS credentials, or remote credit cards. All core calculations, mathematical tools, and data persistence run directly on your machine through Python, Flask, and local SQLite.

Official scheme details, eligibility guidelines, technical specifications, and vendor application portals are published by the Ministry of New and Renewable Energy (MNRE) on the National Portal for PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana at myscheme.gov.in/schemes/pmsgmb.

Financial & Subsidy Disclaimer

All financial figures—including turnkey installation costs (assumed at ₹55,000/kW benchmark), PM Surya Ghar central subsidies (up to ₹78,000 for standard residential systems), monthly savings, and payback periods—are indicative preliminary estimates provided solely for educational demonstration purposes. Actual equipment pricing, labor costs, net-metering approval, feed-in tariffs, and subsidy disbursements depend on state-specific DISCOM regulations, consumer category, empanelled contractor quotes, and Ministry of New and Renewable Energy (MNRE) guidelines. SolarSaathi does not guarantee subsidy approval, exact bill reductions, or financial returns. Users should consult their local DISCOM and qualified solar professionals before making financial investments.

Data, Estimates & Environmental Disclaimer

Calculations in this application rely on regional planning insolation benchmarks (4.1 to 5.8 peak sun hours per day) and a standard 80% system performance ratio. Optional Open-Meteo weather readings provide an educational snapshot of current atmospheric irradiance and are not used as the basis for 25-year financial projections. Environmental conversions—such as 0.82 kg CO₂ avoided per kWh, 0.45 kg coal displaced per kWh, 2.5 litres cooling water conserved per kWh, and 22 kg CO₂ annual absorption per mature tree—are simplified grid-average illustrative benchmarks from Central Electricity Authority (CEA) baselines, not direct site measurements. This application does not replace a professional site shadow-analysis or certified structural engineering survey.
